Four people, two ovens, one very old starter

North Star opened in 2009 in a space that used to be a shoe repair shop. Ruth signed the lease in March, the ovens arrived in April, and the first batch of sourdough went into the case in May with a handwritten price tag because we had not ordered signs yet. Seventeen years later the case is the same one, the price tags are still handwritten, and the starter from that first week is still in the back room.

How we got here

For the first six years the bakery was Ruth and one part-time baker, and we only sold wholesale to two restaurants. The neighborhood kept asking when we would open a counter, so in 2015 we knocked out the wall to the storage room, added four stools, and started selling to whoever walked in. We have grown slowly since then, mostly by hiring people who were already regulars.

The one thing that has not changed is the schedule. Bread is mixed in the afternoon, shaped in the evening, and baked overnight, which is why the ovens are still warm when the first customer arrives.

Where our ingredients come from

Our wheat comes from a farm about ninety miles east, and we buy it through a mill that stone-grinds to order, so the flour is usually a week old when we open the bag. It costs more than the commodity flour we started with, and the bread tastes like something, so we kept it.

Butter, milk, and eggs come from a dairy co-op in the next county. The honey is from a beekeeper who sells at the Saturday market, and in the summer we buy fruit for the thumbprint cookies from the same stalls. In January the jam is whatever we froze in July, which is a thing customers notice and ask about every year.

The people you will see at the counter

Ruth Alvarez

Owner and head baker. Ruth trained as a pastry chef and spent nine years in restaurant kitchens before deciding she was tired of working nights for other people and would rather work nights for herself. She still shapes every loaf of sourdough by hand and is the only person allowed to feed Dot.

Sam Okafor

Baker. Sam came in as a dishwasher in 2018 and worked his way onto the bench. He runs the pastry side now, which means the croissants are his and so is the argument about whether the cinnamon rolls need more cinnamon.

Priya Deshmukh

Counter and orders. Priya handles pre-orders, cake questions, and the phone. If you have ever picked up a sheet cake with your name written neatly on the box, that was her.

Luis Ortega

Saturday counter. Luis remembers most customers' usual order, which is a skill we did not know we needed until we had it.
